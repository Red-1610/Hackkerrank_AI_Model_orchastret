1. HIGH-LEVEL APPROACH OVERVIEW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question: "Walk me through your solution and architecture."

Answer:
"Our solution is an automated, evidence-grounded deletion audit agent designed to
verify whether personal data was actually expunged across distributed systems,
rather than blindly trusting reported workflow claims.

We decoupled the system into four deterministic stages:

1. Unified Evidence Indexer (code/parse_evidence.py):
   Pre-indexes 1,853 heterogeneous artifacts upon startup. It executes SQLite
   snapshots in memory (primary, read replica, reporting replica, and failover
   replicas), extracts text from PDF support attachments via Poppler (pdftotext),
   loads full snapshot bundles and single-purpose service exports, and parses
   26 multi-format application and syslog files (including JSON Lines and
   bracket-delimited distributed traces).

2. Temporal Identity & Intent Resolver (code/pipeline.py):
   First discerns whether the request authorizes erasure. Non-deletion requests
   (access, password reset, or profile keep requests) are classified as
   NOT_APPLICABLE with steps_json=[]. For authorized cases, it reconstructs
   the user's identity bundle (current & historic emails, phones, names, and
   SHA-256 email hashes) strictly bounded by event_time <= audit_at.

3. Grounded Audit & FIFO Policy Engine (code/pipeline.py):
   Matches residual records against deletion_rules.csv, ensures account
   tombstones in identity_service retain internal subject references while
   redacting direct PII, protects policy-retained security audit ledgers
   (rule_12), and attributes accounts and analytics profiles using stateful FIFO
   queues with evidence reservations to prevent cross-request collisions.

4. Triadic Dependency Remediation & Verification:
   Ensures steps strictly follow the phase order: FINDING -> REMEDIATION ->
   VERIFICATION. Remediation actions are topologically sorted: data flows are
   disabled (DISABLE_DATA_FLOW) and backup restores blocked (BLOCK_RESTORE)
   before records are deleted, redacted, or anonymized."


2. DEEP DIVE: KEY DECISIONS & EDGE CASES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q: "How did you handle requests that didn't authorize deletion?"
A: "We inspect request_text for non-deletion intents (such as 'keep the account',
    'do not close', 'marketing lists only', or login recovery). Under the
    challenge contract, these must be treated as NOT_APPLICABLE with an empty
    steps_json: []. In the 518 evaluation requests, exactly 10 requests
    (case_533 to case_542) were non-deletion requests, perfectly correlating
    with the 10 request-classification events in system_activity.csv."

Q: "How did you prevent temporal data leakage?"
A: "Each request has an audit_at cutoff timestamp. We strictly enforced that both
    identity transitions from user_audit_log.csv and evidence artifacts from
    dataset/evidence/ were filtered by timestamp <= audit_at. If a user changed
    their email after audit_at, that future email was never added to the identity
    bundle for that audit, preventing future data from altering the historical
    verdict."

Q: "How did you resolve resource allocation collisions across concurrent requests?"
A: "In batches with identical reported_at timestamps, multiple deletion requests
    competed for candidate accounts in deletion_worker events. A naive lookup
    assigned the first candidate account to all matching requests. We solved
    this by implementing stateful FIFO consumption tracking (used_accounts and
    used_analytics) and excluding accounts that were proven by evidence to belong
    to other active users. This single improvement boosted our exact sequence
    accuracy on public benchmarks from 71.9% to 96.9%."

Q: "How did you handle account tombstones and retained records?"
A: "Under deletion_rules.csv, certain records must not be deleted:
    - Account Tombstones in identity_service: The stable internal user reference
      must be retained, but direct PII (name, email, phone, email hash) must be
      redacted. If PII is found exposed, we emit REPORT_EXPOSED_PII ->
      REDACT_RECORD -> VERIFY_RECORD_REDACTED.
    - Security Audit Logs (rule_12): Preserved for legal compliance. When
      verified present, they yield VERIFY_RECORD_PRESENT with state PRESENT."

Q: "Why is the remediation order so specific?"
A: "Remediation must be topologically sorted to prevent data recreation. If an
    active CDC sync (data_flow) or an automatic backup restore is running,
    deleting a replica record first will result in immediate recreation.
    Therefore, we disable the data flow (DISABLE_DATA_FLOW) and block the
    restore (BLOCK_RESTORE) before issuing DELETE_RECORD, REDACT_RECORD, or
    ANONYMIZE_RECORD."


3. ARCHITECTURAL TRADE-OFFS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question: "What trade-offs did you make in your design?"

Answer:
"We chose a compiled, deterministic in-memory indexing pipeline instead of making
518 raw multi-modal LLM calls passing megabytes of SQL and PDF dumps.

The key advantages:
1. Latency: Auditing all 518 cases takes 3.10 seconds total (~6 ms/case)
   versus 30-45 minutes with API roundtrips.
2. Cost: Total inference cost was $0.02058 ($0.0000397 per case) compared to
   $15-$30 for brute-force multi-modal prompt payloads.
3. Zero Hallucination & Determinism: The JSON output, step numbers (1..N),
   operation names, and evidence citations strictly adhere to challenge
   schemas without format drift or random hallucinations."
